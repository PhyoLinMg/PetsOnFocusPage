In a 5-minute Pomodoro break, get away from your screen and do something physical and easy: stand up, drink water, stretch, look out of a window or walk to another room. Avoid anything that pulls you into a new task, especially your phone. After four work blocks, use the longer 15 to 30 minute break for a proper meal, a walk or a rest.

This guide gives you ideas for both kinds of break, a few things to avoid, and a simple way to choose.

## What makes a good Pomodoro break?

A good Pomodoro break lets your brain rest and your body move, and it has a clear end. Three tests help:

- **Is it different from the work?** If you have been reading, don't spend the break reading something else on a screen.
- **Is it easy to stop?** A walk to the kitchen ends by itself. A video app is built to keep going.
- **Do you come back ready?** If you return more tired or more distracted, the break was the wrong one.

The break is part of the [Pomodoro technique](/guides/pomodoro-technique-for-studying/), not a reward you have to earn. Skipping it usually makes the next block worse.

## What can you do in a 5-minute break?

Pick from this list. Most need nothing but a few minutes.

**Move your body**

1. Stand up and stretch your neck, shoulders and wrists.
2. Walk to another room and back.
3. Do ten slow squats or a minute of gentle movement.
4. Step outside for a breath of fresh air if you can.

**Rest your eyes and mind**

5. Look out of a window at something far away for a minute.
6. Close your eyes and breathe slowly, in for four, out for six.
7. Sit quietly with nothing in your hands.

**Look after yourself**

8. Fill your water bottle and drink some.
9. Eat a small snack, such as fruit or nuts.
10. Make a cup of tea.

**Tiny, finite chores**

11. Water a plant.
12. Put a few things away, clear your desk, or load the dishwasher.
13. Tidy the next block's materials so you can start straight away.

**Something playful**

14. Stroke a pet, a real one if you have one.
15. Put on one song and listen to the whole thing.

Notice what is missing: email, messages, social media and the news. Those can wait for the long break or the end of the day.

## What can you do in a long break?

A long Pomodoro break is 15 to 30 minutes, taken after four work blocks. You have more room, so choose something that truly refills you:

- **Eat a proper meal** or a decent snack.
- **Take a walk**, ideally outside.
- **Lie down for a short rest.** Set an alarm if you worry about dozing off, and keep it short so you can start again.
- **Call or chat to someone** for a few minutes, with a clear end time.
- **Do something with your hands**, such as cooking, a quick tidy or a stretch routine.

If you want to check messages, the long break is the place, with a limit: read them, reply to what is urgent, and stop.

## What should you avoid in a Pomodoro break?

Avoid anything that is hard to stop or that loads your brain with new things.

- **Scrolling your phone.** Five minutes becomes twenty, and your head fills with other people's news. [How to stop checking your phone while studying](/guides/how-to-stop-checking-your-phone-while-studying/) has setups that make this easier.
- **Starting something big.** A "quick" episode, a game or a long chat will run over.
- **Working through it.** Doing a bit more work in the break defeats the point of the break.
- **Making decisions.** Leave planning and big choices for another time.

If you do look at your phone, set a separate timer for it. Better still, leave the phone in another room.

## How do you stop a break running over?

Treat the end of the break as seriously as the end of the work block.

1. **Have the next timer ready.** A Pomodoro timer that starts the next stage for you removes the choice.
2. **Decide the first action of the next block** before you stand up, such as "open question 5".
3. **Choose breaks that end themselves**: finish the glass of water, the stretch, the walk to the end of the road.
4. **Shorten the break if you keep overrunning**, rather than skipping it. A calm 5 minutes beats an anxious 10.

## Should breaks be longer than 5 minutes?

Sometimes. If 5 minutes leaves you tired, or you are doing deeper work such as essay writing, a longer rhythm may suit you better. ## Common questions

### What should I do during a 5-minute Pomodoro break?

Stand up, drink water, stretch and look at something far away. Pick something physical or restful that ends by itself, and stay off your phone.

### Can I use my phone in a Pomodoro break?

It is better not to. Phones are hard to put down, and a 5-minute break easily becomes twenty. If you do use it, set a separate timer, or keep checking messages for the long break.

### Should I keep working if I'm in flow when the timer rings?

You can, but take a break soon after. If you are often in flow when the timer rings, try a longer block instead of ignoring the break.

## In short

- In a 5-minute break: move, drink water, rest your eyes, and keep it screen-free.
- In a long break: eat, walk or rest properly.
- Avoid your phone, big tasks and more work.
- Prepare the next block's first step, and use a timer so the break ends on time.
