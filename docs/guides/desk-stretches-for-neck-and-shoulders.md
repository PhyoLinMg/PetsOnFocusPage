The easiest way to ease neck and shoulder tension at your desk is a gentle five-minute routine: shoulder rolls, a side neck stretch, a slow chin turn, a chest opener and an overhead reach. Hold each stretch for 15 to 30 seconds, breathe slowly, stop if anything hurts, and repeat once an hour or at every break. Stretching should feel like a mild pull, never pain.

This guide gives you the routine step by step, explains why desk work tightens your neck and shoulders, and covers the small changes that make the tension come back less often. It is general information, not medical advice.

## Why do your neck and shoulders get tight at a desk?

Neck and shoulders tighten at a desk because you hold nearly the same position for a long time. Staying still, with your head forward and your shoulders slightly raised, keeps the same muscles working without a rest.

Common triggers:

- Leaning towards the screen, especially a laptop that sits low.
- Shoulders creeping up towards your ears while you concentrate or feel stressed.
- Cradling a phone, or looking down at one.
- Long stretches of focus with no break, because you forget to move.

Moving more often is the simplest fix. Several short moves a day usually beat one long session.

## What is a good 5-minute desk stretch routine?

A good routine has five moves that you can do in your chair, in about a minute each. Sit tall, feet flat on the floor, and breathe slowly throughout.

1. **Shoulder shrugs and rolls (about 10 each).** Lift both shoulders towards your ears, hold for a second, then let them drop. Then roll them backwards in slow circles.
2. **Side neck stretch (15 to 30 seconds per side).** Let your right ear drift towards your right shoulder. Keep the left shoulder down. Use no force. Change sides.
3. **Slow chin turn (5 times per side).** Keep your shoulders still and turn your head gently to look over one shoulder. Come back to the middle, then turn the other way.
4. **Chest opener (15 to 30 seconds).** Clasp your hands behind your back, or hold the sides of your chair, and gently lift your chest. This opens the front of your shoulders, which round forward when you type.
5. **Overhead reach (15 to 30 seconds).** Interlace your fingers, turn your palms up, and reach up and slightly back. Breathe out as you stretch.

### How should a stretch feel?

A stretch should feel like a mild, even pull that eases as you hold it. Stop if you feel sharp pain, pins and needles, numbness or dizziness. Move slowly, never bounce, and never push your head with your hands.

## How often should you stretch at your desk?

Stretch for one to two minutes every 30 to 60 minutes of desk work, and do the full routine once or twice a day. Frequent short stretches help more than one long stretch after hours of sitting.

A simple pattern:

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Every 30 to 60 minutes | Shrugs and a side neck stretch for one to two minutes |
| At each longer break | The full five-minute routine |
| End of the work day | Gentle walk and the chest opener |

Do not rely on stretching alone to fix a set-up that strains you. See the next section.

## What else reduces neck and shoulder tension?

Small changes to how you sit and how long you stay still often matter as much as stretches. Try these first:

- **Raise the screen** so the top of it is roughly at eye level. On a laptop, use a stand and a separate keyboard.
- **Keep your elbows close to your sides** and your forearms supported, so your shoulders can relax.
- **Use a headset** instead of tucking a phone under your chin.
- **Stand up regularly.** Walking to refill your water does more than another stretch.
- **Notice your shoulders.** Every time you check the clock, ask: are they up near my ears? Drop them.

## How do you remember to stretch?

You remember by letting something else interrupt you, because deep focus makes you forget your body. Tie the stretch to a break, not to willpower.

1. Work in blocks of 25 to 50 minutes, with a timer ending each one.
2. Make the first thing you do in every break a 60-second stretch.
3. Keep the break away from your phone. [What to do in a Pomodoro break](/guides/what-to-do-in-a-pomodoro-break/) has more ideas for filling a break that rests you.

## When should you see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if pain is severe, lasts more than a few weeks, keeps getting worse, or comes with numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands. Get urgent medical help if neck pain follows an injury, or comes with a severe headache, fever, or trouble with balance or speech. If you have a known neck or shoulder injury, ask a professional before stretching.

## Common questions

### How long should you hold a neck stretch?

Hold a gentle neck stretch for 15 to 30 seconds on each side. Breathe slowly, keep the stretch mild and stop if it hurts.

### Is it OK to stretch my neck every day?

For most people, gentle neck stretches done a few times a day are fine. If you have an injury, a health condition or pain that does not settle, check with a doctor or physiotherapist first.

### Why do my shoulders hurt after a day at the computer?

Holding one position for hours keeps the same muscles working, and shoulders often creep upwards when you concentrate. Regular movement, a better screen height and supported forearms usually help.

### Should I stretch before or after work?

Do both if you like, but the stretches that matter most are the short ones during the day, because that is when you hold the position. A short walk and a few shrugs at each break are enough.

## In short

- Do five gentle moves: shrugs, side neck stretch, chin turn, chest opener, overhead reach.
- Hold each for 15 to 30 seconds and never push into pain.
- Stretch for a minute or two every 30 to 60 minutes.
- Raise your screen, support your arms and take breaks.
- See a doctor if pain is severe, lasts weeks or comes with numbness.
