To beat an afternoon slump at work, move for five minutes, get bright light or fresh air, drink some water, and switch to lighter tasks in 15 to 25 minute focus blocks. Then plan tomorrow so your hardest work sits in the morning and your admin sits in the dip. Skipping the cause (too little sleep, no real lunch) is what makes the slump come back every day.

An afternoon slump is the dip in energy and attention many people feel in the early to mid afternoon, often a few hours after lunch. It is common, and it is not a sign that you are lazy. This guide covers what helps right now, and how to arrange your day so the dip costs you less.

## Why do you get an afternoon slump?

The afternoon slump usually comes from a mix of ordinary things rather than one cause. Check which of these sound like your day:

| Possible cause | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Short sleep the night before | Heavy eyes from about 2 pm, however much coffee you drink |
| A big or rushed lunch | Drowsy within an hour of eating |
| No lunch break | Running on empty, then grabbing something sugary |
| Sitting still for hours | Stiff, restless and sleepy at the same time |
| Dim, warm or stuffy room | Sleepier the longer you sit there |
| Hours of hard thinking already done | Flat even on easy tasks |

Often two or three overlap. Start with the cheapest fix.

## What helps in the next ten minutes?

Change something physical for two to five minutes, then go back to a short block. Movement and light are the quickest resets, because they change your body state rather than asking your willpower to work harder.

- **Stand up and walk.** Go to another room, up and down some stairs, or round the block.
- **Get light.** Sit by a window, open a blind or step outside for a few minutes.
- **Drink water.** Keep a glass on the desk and refill it each break.
- **Stretch.** Roll your shoulders, open your chest, stand and reach.
- **Change the task.** Swap a long document for something active, like replying to a short list of messages or sorting files.
- **Cool the room.** A stuffy room makes everyone sleepier; open a window if you can.

If none of this touches it and you keep reading the same line, you are likely past a useful push. Take a proper break, then choose an easier job.

## How should you set up work around the dip?

Put demanding work in the part of the day when you feel sharpest, and keep routine tasks for the dip. Most people can tell when they are at their best; for many it is the morning, though yours may differ.

A simple split:

1. **Your sharp hours:** writing, problem-solving, learning, anything you need to think hard about.
2. **The dip:** email, admin, tidying documents, expenses, planning tomorrow, short meetings.
3. **Late afternoon:** a second, smaller window for a medium task as energy returns.

Write tomorrow's two or three most important tasks before you finish today. That way the morning starts on the hard thing instead of on email. [Time blocking vs Pomodoro](/guides/time-blocking-vs-pomodoro/) shows how to put tasks into fixed slots.

## How long should focus blocks be in the afternoon?

Shorter than your morning blocks: 15 to 25 minutes of focus followed by a real five-minute break is a good starting point. A shorter block makes starting easier when you feel flat, and the end is always close.

- Try a 20-minute block on one clear task.
- Take the break away from the screen, and make it physical: stand, walk, look out of a window.
- Do two or three blocks, then reassess. If you are still struggling, take a longer break.

[How many Pomodoros a day is enough?](/guides/how-many-pomodoros-a-day/) helps you decide when to stop pushing, and [what to do in a Pomodoro break](/guides/what-to-do-in-a-pomodoro-break/) has break ideas that rest you rather than pull you into your phone.

## What should you do about lunch, caffeine and naps?

Take a real lunch, eat something that keeps you comfortable rather than stuffed, and be careful with caffeine late in the day.

- **Lunch.** Skipping it often backfires. A seated meal away from your screen, with a short walk afterwards, is a better reset than eating at your desk. [A real lunch break when you work from home](/guides/lunch-break-when-working-from-home/) has a simple routine.
- **Caffeine.** It takes time to work and stays in your body for hours, so a late coffee can cost you sleep tonight and worsen tomorrow's dip. People vary in how sensitive they are; notice your own pattern.
- **Naps.** If you work from home and can lie down, a short nap of around 20 minutes with an alarm suits many people better than a long one, which can leave you groggy. Don't nap late in the afternoon if it delays your bedtime.

None of these replaces sleep. If the slump is heavy every day, look at your sleep first. [How to stop feeling tired while studying](/guides/how-to-stop-feeling-tired-while-studying/) goes through sleep, food and movement in more detail, and the advice fits desk work too.

## When is an afternoon slump a sign of something else?

If you feel exhausted most days for weeks, even after enough sleep, or you also have low mood, unusual thirst, breathlessness or other symptoms, speak to a doctor. An ordinary afternoon dip comes and goes with your day; ongoing exhaustion deserves a proper check.

## Common questions

### What time is the afternoon slump?

For many people it falls in the early to mid afternoon, often a few hours after lunch. The exact time varies with your sleep, your meals and your body clock.

### Does eating lunch cause the afternoon slump?

Lunch can make a dip feel stronger, especially a very large meal, but the dip also happens to people who skip lunch. Eating a moderate meal and walking afterwards usually helps.

### Is coffee a good fix for the afternoon slump?

A cup can help in the short term. Having it late, or relying on it daily, can cut into your sleep, which makes the next slump worse.

### Is it normal to feel sleepy at work after lunch?

Yes, it is very common. It becomes worth a doctor's visit if you are exhausted most days or it is getting worse.

## In short

- Move, get light and drink water for a few minutes when the dip hits.
- Do routine tasks in the dip and hard thinking in your sharpest hours.
- Use shorter 15 to 25 minute blocks with real breaks in the afternoon.
- Take a proper lunch, watch late caffeine and protect your sleep.
- If you are exhausted for weeks, see a doctor.
