A weekly review is a 20-minute slot, once a week, where you look back at what happened, deal with loose ends, check the week ahead and choose three priorities. Do it at the same time each week, work from a short checklist, and decide about every item rather than doing the work itself.

This guide gives you a four-step method, a minute-by-minute plan, a template to copy and fixes for the usual ways reviews go wrong.

## What is a weekly review?

A weekly review is a short, regular session where you step back from the daily rush and ask what happened, what is unfinished and what matters next. It is a planning habit, not a work session.

Its job is to stop small things piling up. Without one, unanswered messages, half-finished tasks and vague "I should" thoughts sit in your head and make the week feel heavier than it is. Writing them down and deciding about each one lets you start Monday knowing what comes first.

## How do you do a weekly review in 20 minutes?

Work through four steps in order: capture, look back, look ahead, choose. Set a timer and stop when it rings.

| Minutes | Step | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Capture | Write down every loose task, promise and worry on one list. |
| 4–9 | Look back | Scan last week for unfinished tasks and follow-ups. |
| 9–15 | Look ahead | Check next week's calendar, deadlines and preparation. |
| 15–20 | Choose | Pick three priorities and give each a day. |

Before you start, open four things: your calendar, your task list, last week's notes and a blank page. Searching for them mid-review is how 20 minutes becomes an hour.

### Step 1: Capture (about 4 minutes)

Empty your head and your pockets onto one list. Include scraps of paper, open browser tabs, notes on your phone and things you promised people. Do not sort yet. The point is to get everything in one place.

### Step 2: Look back (about 5 minutes)

Scan the week that just ended and ask three questions:

- What did I finish? Write down two or three wins, however small.
- What did I start and not finish?
- Who is waiting for something from me, or I from them?

Move anything unfinished onto your capture list. Wins matter here: a list of what you did is a better mirror than a list of what remains.

### Step 3: Look ahead (about 6 minutes)

Open next week's calendar and look for three things:

- **Fixed commitments:** meetings, classes, deadlines.
- **Preparation:** anything that needs work before an event, such as slides for Thursday.
- **Gaps:** blocks of free time long enough for focused work.

Put preparation tasks on the day they should start, not the day they are due. The gaps are where your priorities will go.

### Step 4: Choose (about 5 minutes)

Go through the capture list and give every item one decision:

- **Do:** schedule it into a specific gap next week.
- **Defer:** put it on a "later" list with a review date.
- **Drop:** delete it. If it has sat on your list for a month, you probably don't need it.
- **Hand over:** ask someone else, or note who you are waiting on.

Then choose three priorities for the week. These are the three outcomes that would make the week a good one. Everything else is a bonus.

## What is a good weekly review template?

A template keeps the review short because you never wonder what to do next. Copy this and fill it in each week:

1. **Wins:** two or three things I finished.
2. **Loose ends:** things I started but didn't finish, and who is waiting on me.
3. **Next week's fixed points:** meetings, deadlines, events.
4. **Preparation needed:** what must start before it is due.
5. **My three priorities:** and the day I will work on each.
6. **One thing to stop, one thing to start:** a small change for next week.

If 20 minutes feels like too much to begin with, use only lines 1, 5 and 6 for the first few weeks. A review you actually do beats a thorough one you skip.

## When is the best time for a weekly review?

The best time is a fixed slot you can protect: Friday afternoon, when you close out the week, or Sunday evening, when you plan the next one. Friday leaves your weekend free of loose ends; Sunday means you start Monday with a plan.

Pick one and attach it to something you already do, such as the last coffee on Friday. Putting it in the calendar as a repeating event makes it a habit, not a decision you have to make each week.

## What are common weekly review mistakes?

The usual mistakes are doing the work during the review, leaving maybes undecided and skipping the review after a bad week.

- **Doing, not deciding.** If a task takes under two minutes, do it; otherwise record the next action and move on.
- **Leaving maybes.** Every item gets a decision. "Maybe" is how lists grow.
- **Too many priorities.** More than three and none of them is a priority.
- **Skipping after a bad week.** A rough week is when you most need a short, kind review. Do the three-line version.
- **Making it a guilt trip.** The review is to learn, not to punish. Write what to change, then close the page.

## Common questions

### How long should a weekly review take?

Twenty minutes is enough for most people once they have a template. A first review may take longer because you are gathering loose items; later ones get faster.

### What day is best for a weekly review?

Friday afternoon or Sunday evening suit most people. Choose the one you are least likely to skip and keep it the same every week.

### What should I include in a weekly review?

Include what you finished, what is unfinished, next week's fixed commitments, any preparation and three priorities. Add one thing to stop and one to start if you want a small improvement each week.

### Is a weekly review worth it if I'm busy?

Yes, because it is most useful when you are busy. Twenty minutes spent choosing priorities usually saves time that would otherwise go on switching between tasks and remembering what you forgot.

## In short

- Hold a 20-minute review at the same time each week.
- Follow four steps: capture, look back, look ahead, choose.
- Decide about every item; do the work later.
- End with three priorities, each given a day.
- Time it, so it stays 20 minutes.
