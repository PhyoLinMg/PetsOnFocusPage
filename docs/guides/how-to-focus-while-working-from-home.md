To focus while working from home, give work one place, decide your top task before you start, work in timed sessions of 25 to 50 minutes with real breaks, keep your phone out of reach, and tell the people you live with when you need quiet. Home has no built-in start or finish, so the structure has to come from you.

This guide gives nine habits, a sample day, and fixes for the most common home distractions.

## Why is it hard to focus at home?

Home is built for rest, chores and people, so almost everything in it competes with work. At an office, the place, the colleagues and the commute all signal "work now". At home you have to create those signals yourself. That is why a few small rules help more than willpower.

## What helps you focus while working from home?

Nine habits cover most of it. Start with the first three.

### 1. Give work one place

Work in the same spot each day, ideally a table or desk rather than your bed or sofa. If you only have a shared table, make a small ritual: clear it, open your laptop, put your notebook on the left. Your brain learns the pattern, and the end of the ritual is the end of work.

### 2. Decide your top task the night before

Write down the one to three things that matter most tomorrow. Starting the day with "what should I do?" is when email and messages win. Make the first task small and specific, such as "draft the intro of the report", not "work on the report".

### 3. Work in timed sessions

A timer turns a vague day into short, clear stretches. The [Pomodoro technique](/guides/pomodoro-technique-for-studying/) uses 25 minutes of work and a 5-minute break; many people doing deeper work prefer 50 minutes. [How long should a focus session be?](/guides/how-long-should-a-focus-session-be/) helps you choose. Whichever you pick, stop when the timer says, because the break is what keeps the afternoon going.

### 4. Keep your phone out of reach

Your phone is the biggest distraction at home, because nobody is watching. Put it in another room, or at least face down and out of arm's reach, during a session. [How to stop checking your phone while studying](/guides/how-to-stop-checking-your-phone-while-studying/) has setups that work just as well for a desk job.

### 5. Close the tabs you don't need

Keep only what the current task needs. Mute chat and email notifications during sessions, and check them at set times, such as the end of each session or twice an hour.

### 6. Tell the people you live with

Others cannot read your mind. Agree simple signals: headphones on means "please don't interrupt", a closed door means "urgent only". Tell them when you will be free, so waiting is easy.

### 7. Take real breaks, away from the desk

Stand up, drink water, look out of a window, and step away from your screen. Scrolling is not a break. [What to do in a Pomodoro break](/guides/what-to-do-in-a-pomodoro-break/) has ideas for both short and long breaks.

### 8. Choose your sound

If your home is noisy, or too quiet, steady sound helps some people. Rain, a café hum or instrumental music are common choices; lyrics tend to be harder when you are writing or reading. [Best sounds for focus](/guides/best-sounds-for-focus/) compares the options.

### 9. End the day on purpose

Pick a finishing time and a closing ritual: write tomorrow's list, close the laptop, put it away. Without an end, work leaks into the evening and you feel unfocused all day because you are never fully off.

## What does a focused work-from-home day look like?

Here is a simple pattern. Change the times to suit you.

| Time | What |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Clear the desk, review the top task list |
| First block | 25 to 50 minutes on the hardest task, phone away |
| Break | 5 to 10 minutes away from the screen |
| Blocks 2 to 4 | Continue the main work, then a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes |
| Midday | Proper lunch, away from the desk |
| Afternoon | Meetings, messages and lighter work |
| End | Write tomorrow's list, shut the laptop |

Put your hardest work in the part of the day when you feel most alert, and your messages and admin in the dips.

## How do you handle common home distractions?

- **Chores.** Write a "later" list. Do a two-minute chore in a break if you like, but not in the middle of a session.
- **Housemates or children.** Agree on quiet times in advance, and use signals rather than arguments. If you can't get a quiet block, aim for several shorter ones.
- **The fridge.** Plan snacks and lunch in your breaks so you aren't grazing between tasks.
- **Loneliness.** Some people find it easier to work with another person nearby, even in silence, or with a "study with me" style video. [Study with me: how to recreate it on your own](/guides/study-with-me-how-to-recreate-it-on-your-own/) explains the idea.
- **Feeling stuck at the start.** Do five minutes on the first small step. Starting is usually the hard part.

You can also write what you are working on and pin it before you start, so the task is in front of you when the timer begins.

## Common questions

### How do I stay focused when working from home with kids?

Plan for shorter blocks. Agree quiet times with another adult if you can, use signals the children understand, and save your most demanding task for the time you are least likely to be interrupted.

### What is the best way to start a work-from-home day?

Begin with a short routine: clear your desk, look at your top task for the day, and start a timed session on the first small step before you open email.

### How long should I work before a break at home?

Most people do well with 25 to 50 minutes of work followed by a 5 to 10 minute break. Try 25 first, and lengthen the block if you keep getting pulled out of flow by the timer.

## In short

- Give work one place, one top task and a clear start.
- Work in timed sessions of 25 to 50 minutes, with real breaks away from the screen.
- Keep your phone out of reach and silence what you don't need.
- Tell the people around you when you need quiet.
- Finish on purpose, so work and home stay separate.
