To start studying when you don't feel like it, make the first step tiny and give yourself a short deadline: sit down, open one page, and promise yourself 5 minutes with a timer running. You don't need to feel ready first. Feeling like it usually arrives after you begin, not before.

This guide gives you a simple starting routine, ways to shrink the first step, and what to do if the five minutes ends and you still want to stop.

## Why is starting so hard?

Starting is hard because the work looks big and the reward is far away. Your brain compares "read a whole chapter" with "do anything else" and picks anything else. Waiting to feel motivated doesn't fix that, because motivation is unreliable. A starting routine that works on a flat day beats a plan that needs a good mood.

The trick is to make the start so small that it is hard to say no to.

## What is the five-minute start?

The five-minute start is a promise to study for just 5 minutes, with permission to stop afterwards. It works because the hard part is beginning, not continuing.

1. Pick one specific task (see below).
2. Set a timer for 5 minutes.
3. Work until it rings.
4. Decide: stop, or run another 20 minutes.

Most of the time you will carry on, because you are already in the work. If you really do stop, you still did 5 minutes, which counts. Don't make stopping a failure, or the trick stops working.

## How do you make the first step smaller?

Shrink the task until it feels almost silly. "Study chemistry" is a mountain; these are steps:

- Open the textbook at the right page.
- Read the first paragraph.
- Do one practice question.
- Write the heading of today's notes.
- Copy out one formula from memory, then check it.

Write the task on paper or in your timer app before you begin. A task with a clear end also tells you when you have finished, which is a small reward.

## What if you can't decide what to study first?

Start with something easy that you already half-know, such as flashcards or tidying yesterday's notes. This warms you up and gets the habit of sitting down going. Move to the harder material once you are settled. If the hard thing is the thing you are avoiding, do the five-minute start on it first and see how it feels.

## How do you make starting automatic?

Make the start the same every day, so you aren't asking "do I feel ready?" each time.

- **Same time:** after a meal, or straight after class.
- **Same place:** a desk or table, not your bed.
- **Same first action:** water, phone out of the room, notes open, timer on.

After a week or two, the routine does the deciding for you. ## What if the five minutes ends and you still can't focus?

Check the basics before blaming yourself. You may be tired, hungry or working on a task that is still too vague. [Why you can't focus on studying](/guides/why-you-cant-focus-on-studying/) walks through the common causes and a fix for each. If you are worn out, resting is a better choice than forcing it.

If you are fine but just flat, lengthen the block gently to 15 or 25 minutes. The [Pomodoro technique for studying](/guides/pomodoro-technique-for-studying/) gives you a rhythm of focus and break, and [how long a focus session should be](/guides/how-long-should-a-focus-session-be/) helps you find your own length.

## Common questions

### How do I make myself study when I have no motivation?

Don't wait for motivation. Pick one small task, set a timer for 5 minutes and begin. Motivation often follows once you are already working.

### What do I do when I can't start studying?

Make the first step smaller until it feels easy, such as opening the notes or doing one question. Then run a short timer and let yourself stop when it ends.

### Is it normal not to feel like studying?

Yes. Almost everyone has days when studying feels unappealing. A routine that doesn't depend on mood gets you through them. If low motivation lasts for weeks or comes with low mood or constant worry, talk to a doctor or a support service at your school or university.

## In short

- Don't wait to feel like it; start small.
- Promise yourself 5 minutes with a timer, and let yourself stop afterwards.
- Shrink the first step: open the page, do one question.
- Use the same time, place and first action every day.
- If you still can't focus, check tiredness and vague tasks first.
