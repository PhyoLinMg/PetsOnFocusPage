A deep work session is a block of 50 to 90 minutes spent on one demanding task with your phone away and notifications off. To plan one, pick a single task, choose a start time and length, remove distractions beforehand, work without switching, and finish with a short break. Do one or two a day, not eight.

The term comes from Cal Newport's book *Deep Work*: focused effort on a cognitively demanding task, free of distraction. This guide turns the idea into a plan you can use today.

## What counts as a deep work session?

A deep work session is one task that needs real thinking, done in one uninterrupted block. Writing an essay, solving problem sets, coding a hard feature or reading a dense chapter all qualify. Answering messages, filing receipts and tidying a spreadsheet are shallow work: useful, but easy to do while half-distracted.

If you could do it while chatting, it isn't deep work. Save it for between sessions.

## How do you plan a deep work session?

Plan it in six steps, ideally the evening before.

1. **Pick one task.** Make it specific: "draft section 2 of the report", not "work on the report".
2. **Pick a time.** Choose the part of the day when you are most alert and put it in your calendar like a meeting.
3. **Pick a length.** Start with 50 minutes. Beginners can start at 25; experienced focusers may stretch to 90.
4. **Decide what "done" looks like.** One page drafted, ten questions solved, one chapter read.
5. **Set up the space.** Water, notes, the files you need open. Everything else closed.
6. **Park the distractions.** Keep a scrap of paper beside you. When a stray thought or errand appears, write it down and go back.

## How long should a deep work session be?

Most people do well with 50 to 90 minutes. Your first sessions can be shorter, and that is fine: the skill grows with practice. ## How many deep work sessions can you do in a day?

One to three is realistic for most people. Deep work is tiring, and quality drops when you push on. Two good sessions in the morning beat five tired ones across the day. Put admin, email and meetings around them.

## What should you do before a session starts?

Spend five minutes on a short start-up routine. It tells your brain that something different is about to happen.

- Phone out of reach, ideally in another room. [How to stop checking your phone while studying](/guides/how-to-stop-checking-your-phone-while-studying/) has setups that work.
- Notifications off on your computer.
- Only the tabs and files for this task open.
- Sound chosen: silence, steady rain or instrumental music. [Best sounds for focus](/guides/best-sounds-for-focus/) compares them.
- Timer started.

## What do you do when your mind wanders?

Notice it, write down the distraction if it matters, and return to the task. Wandering is normal and does not mean the session failed. The goal is not perfect attention but a quick return.

If you keep getting pulled away within a few minutes, the task may be too big or too vague. Shrink it to the next small step and restart.

## How should you end a deep work session?

End on the timer, then spend two minutes closing it properly:

- Write one line on where you stopped and what comes next.
- Note anything from your parking-lot list that needs action.
- Take a real break away from the screen: stand, stretch, drink water.

That last line makes the next session easy to start, because you are never beginning from a blank page.

## Common questions

### Is deep work the same as the Pomodoro technique?

No. The Pomodoro technique uses 25-minute work blocks with short breaks, while deep work describes any long, undistracted block on a demanding task. A 25-minute Pomodoro can be deep work if you stay on one hard task, and many people chain two or three.

### How do I start deep work if I get distracted easily?

Begin with 15 to 25 minutes and a very small task, with your phone in another room. Lengthen the session only when you finish most sessions without leaving.

### What is the best time of day for deep work?

The time when you feel most alert, which is often the morning but varies by person. Protect that slot and put shallow tasks in your low-energy hours.

## In short

- A deep work session is 50 to 90 minutes on one demanding task, with no interruptions.
- Plan the task, time, length and finish line beforehand, ideally the night before.
- Remove distractions first, and write down stray thoughts instead of acting on them.
- Do one to three sessions a day, with real breaks between them.
- End by noting where you stopped, so the next session starts easily.
