To take a proper lunch break when you work from home, pick a fixed time, finish a small stopping point, leave your desk, eat somewhere that isn't your workspace, and spend at least 20 to 30 minutes away from screens. Then write down the first task for the afternoon so restarting is easy.

At home nobody sees you leave for lunch, and the kitchen is ten steps away, so lunch often turns into a sandwich eaten over the keyboard. This guide gives a short routine, ideas for the break, and fixes for the usual reasons people skip it.

## Why does lunch get skipped when you work from home?

Lunch gets skipped at home because nothing in the day signals "stop now". In an office, colleagues heading out, a canteen queue or a meeting-free hour mark the middle of the day. At home you have to create that mark yourself.

Common reasons:

- **No cue.** Without a fixed time, "just one more email" wins.
- **Guilt.** Some people feel they should look busy and online.
- **Momentum.** You are in the middle of something and stopping feels costly.
- **Convenience.** Food is close, so eating at the desk feels like a shortcut.

None of these is a character flaw. They are all fixed by making lunch a scheduled event rather than something you remember.

## How do you take a proper lunch break at home?

Follow five steps. They take about two minutes of planning.

1. **Choose a time and put it in your calendar.** Same time each day, if your job allows. A repeating "Lunch" block that others can see also stops meetings being booked over it.
2. **Set an alarm for ten minutes before.** Use it to reach a stopping point, not to start something new.
3. **Leave a note for yourself.** Write the very next step of your current task, so you can pick it up after lunch without thinking.
4. **Leave the desk.** Close the laptop lid or lock the screen. Eat at the table, on the sofa or outside, anywhere except your workspace.
5. **Set the end.** Decide when you are back, and set a timer for it.

## What should you do on a lunch break?

Do something that eats up your time and gives your brain a rest from the work: eat, move, and have a little time away from screens. A good lunch break usually has three parts.

| Part | Time | Ideas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eat | 10 to 20 minutes | Sit down, put your phone away, eat slowly |
| Move | 10 to 15 minutes | A walk round the block, stretching, watering the garden |
| Switch off | 5 to 10 minutes | Read a few pages of a book, listen to music, look out of the window |

If you have less time, keep the first two parts. If you have more, add something you enjoy, such as a call with a friend or cooking something properly. For shorter ideas, [what to do in a Pomodoro break](/guides/what-to-do-in-a-pomodoro-break/) has a long list that works for lunch too.

Try to avoid replying to messages, scrolling the news or starting chores that run over. Those are work and noise in disguise, and you come back no more rested than you left.

## How does lunch fit with focus sessions?

Lunch is the long break of the day. If you work in timed sessions, the usual pattern is a few focus blocks with short breaks, then a long one. [How to focus while working from home](/guides/how-to-focus-while-working-from-home/) puts a proper lunch in the middle of a sample day, and [time blocking vs Pomodoro](/guides/time-blocking-vs-pomodoro/) shows how to plan blocks around fixed points like lunch.

Two tips help:

- **Finish a block before lunch, don't cut one short.** Ending on a natural stopping point means you aren't carrying half a thought into your meal.
- **Start the afternoon with something small.** The first block after lunch is often the sleepiest. Begin with a task you already know how to start, using your note from earlier.

If you hit the middle of the afternoon feeling flat, [how to study for long hours without burning out](/guides/how-to-study-for-long-hours-without-burning-out/) covers pacing a long day, and the ideas apply to desk work as well as study.

## Common questions

### How long should a lunch break be when working from home?

Aim for 30 minutes to an hour, or whatever your contract and workplace agree. Even 20 to 30 minutes away from your desk is better than eating while you work.

### Is it okay to eat at your desk when working from home?

Occasionally, yes. As a habit, it makes the day blur together, because work and rest happen in the same place. Moving to another spot, even the other side of the room, helps.

### What if my team expects me to be online at lunch?

Block the time in your calendar and say when you will be back. A short status message such as "Back at 1:00" is usually enough, and most colleagues respect a clearly marked break.

### Should I take a walk at lunch?

A short walk is one of the easiest ways to mark the middle of the day. If the weather is bad, stretching or moving between rooms still gives your body a change.

## In short

- Fix a lunch time and put it in your calendar.
- Reach a stopping point, and leave a note with your next step.
- Leave your desk, eat away from the screen and move a little.
- Set a timer for your return and start the afternoon with something small.
