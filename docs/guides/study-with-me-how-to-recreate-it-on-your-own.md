"Study with me" works because working next to someone makes it easier to start and harder to drift. You can get the same effect without a video: sit in a library or café, agree a timed session with a friend, or use a visible timer, a quiet sound and one clear task. The ingredients are company, a fixed block of time, and a rule that you stay put until it ends.

This guide breaks down what a study-with-me video gives you, then shows five ways to recreate each part on your own.

## What is a "study with me" video?

A "study with me" video is a recording of someone studying quietly, usually with a timer on screen, so you can work alongside them. Many follow the [Pomodoro technique](/guides/pomodoro-technique-for-studying/): 25 minutes of work, then a 5-minute break. Some run for hours with ambient sound or soft music.

Working next to another person, in person or online, is often called body doubling: someone else being present while you each do your own work.

## Why do they help?

Study-with-me sessions bundle four small helps:

- **Company.** Someone else is working, so working feels normal rather than lonely.
- **A fixed block.** The timer starts and ends for you, so you don't decide moment by moment.
- **A start signal.** Pressing play is a clear "now".
- **Gentle accountability.** Leaving mid-block feels a bit awkward, even when the person can't see you.

None of these needs a screen. You can build each one yourself.

## How can you study alongside someone without a video?

Pick the option that fits your day.

1. **Go where people are working.** A library, a university study room or a quiet café gives you the same passive company. You don't talk; you just share the room.
2. **Book a session with a friend.** Agree a time, say what each of you will work on, then work in silence for one block. Check in at the break. A call with the camera on or off both work.
3. **Study with a housemate or family member.** Sit at the same table with your own work. Ask for one thing only: that they stay in the room until the block ends.
4. **Tell someone your plan.** Message a friend "doing 25 minutes on chapter 3, back at 4:30". Even without them working, you have said it out loud.
5. **Use a timer and a pet.** If nobody is free, a visible countdown and a calm companion give you a start signal and a fixed block.

## How do you set up a solo study-with-me session?

A solo session needs four things, ready before you start.

1. **One task, written down.** "Chapter 3 summary", not "study". If starting is the hard part, [how to start studying when you don't feel like it](/guides/how-to-start-studying-when-you-dont-feel-like-it/) has smaller first steps.
2. **A block length.** 25 minutes is the classic. [How long should a focus session be?](/guides/how-long-should-a-focus-session-be/) helps you choose.
3. **A steady sound, or none.** Rain, a fireplace or a café hum can stand in for a room full of people. [Sounds for focus](/guides/best-sounds-for-focus/) explains how to pick.
4. **Your phone out of reach.** The video is often a way back to the phone. [How to stop checking your phone while studying](/guides/how-to-stop-checking-your-phone-while-studying/) covers setups that work.

## Can a focus app stand in for a study buddy?

Partly. An app can give you the fixed block and the start signal, and a pet can give you something that feels like company. It can't replace a real person if what you need is another human nearby.

Pets on Focus, a focus timer and Pomodoro app for Android, iPhone and iPad, with a pet who keeps you company, shows your pet sitting, napping or stretching while you work, and celebrating when you finish. Focus sessions run from 5 minutes to 4 hours, or you can use Pomodoro cycles with breaks. You can write the task you are working on and pin it before you begin, and mix ambient sounds such as rain, a fireplace, a café or cat purring.

If you leave the app for more than 10 seconds during a session, the session ends without a reward, and you get a notification the moment you leave. It is a nudge to stay, not an app blocker.

## What are the downsides of study-with-me videos?

Videos are not always the best answer.

- **They are on a screen,** next to everything else on the screen.
- **They can become the activity.** Watching someone else study is not studying.
- **Long streams invite skipping breaks,** which you need.

If you use one, put it on a second device or play only the audio, and keep your work on paper or in a single window.

## Common questions

### Do study-with-me videos actually work?

Many people find they help them start and stay seated, mostly through company and a fixed timer. They are not a magic fix, and some people find them distracting, so try one block and see.

### What is body doubling?

Body doubling means working alongside another person, in the same room or online, while each of you does your own task. The other person doesn't need to help; being there is the point.

### How do I study with a friend online?

Pick a time, tell each other what you will work on, then work quietly for one block on a call. Chat only at the break.

## In short

- "Study with me" gives you company, a fixed block and a clear start.
- You can recreate that in a library, with a friend, or alone with a timer.
- Write one task, set a block, keep your phone away, and stay until the timer ends.
- Use a video only if it stays in the background.
