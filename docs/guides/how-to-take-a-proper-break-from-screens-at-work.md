A proper screen break at work is two to five minutes away from every screen, including your phone, every 20 to 30 minutes of focused work, with a longer 10 to 15 minute break once an hour. Stand up, look at something far away, move your body and drink some water. Swapping your work screen for your phone screen is not a break.

This guide explains what makes a screen break count, how often to take one, what to do in it, and how to remember to take it when you are busy.

## What counts as a proper screen break?

A proper screen break has three parts: your eyes look at something far away, your body moves, and no screen replaces the one you left. If all three are true, your eyes, back and attention all get something back.

A quick test:

| Break | Counts? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Looking out of a window for a minute | Yes | Eyes rest on something far away |
| Walking to the kitchen for water | Yes | You move and look at the room |
| Scrolling your phone | No | Same close-up focus, new things to read |
| Switching to email or the news | No | Still a screen, and still work-shaped |
| Watching one video "to switch off" | No | Hard to stop, loads your head with more |

Phones are the usual culprit. [How to stop checking your phone while studying](/guides/how-to-stop-checking-your-phone-while-studying/) has ways to keep yours out of reach during a break.

## How often should you take a screen break?

Take a short look-away break every 20 to 30 minutes, and a longer break once an hour. Nobody can give you one exact right number, so treat these as a starting point and adjust to how your eyes and head feel.

- **Every 20 to 30 minutes:** look away from the screen at something far away for at least 20 seconds, and blink on purpose. This is the idea behind the popular "20-20-20" rule of thumb: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds.
- **Every hour:** stand up, walk and stretch for 5 to 10 minutes.
- **After 90 minutes to 2 hours:** take a proper rest of 15 minutes or more, away from the desk.

Short, frequent breaks suit most desk work better than one long break late in the day. If you already work in focus blocks, the breaks fit naturally between them. [How long should a focus session be?](/guides/how-long-should-a-focus-session-be/) covers block lengths.

## What should you do in a screen break?

Do something that rests your eyes, moves your body and ends by itself. Pick from this list:

**Rest your eyes**

1. Look out of a window at the furthest thing you can see.
2. Close your eyes for 20 to 30 seconds.
3. Blink slowly a few times. People tend to blink less when they stare at a screen, so make it deliberate.

**Move**

4. Stand up and roll your shoulders, then stretch your neck, wrists and back.
5. Walk to another room, or up and down a corridor.
6. Climb a flight of stairs.

**Look after yourself**

7. Fill a glass of water and drink it.
8. Step outside for fresh air, if you can.

**Keep it finite**

9. Water a plant.
10. Tidy the first thing you will need after the break.

For a longer list built around timer breaks, see [what to do in a Pomodoro break](/guides/what-to-do-in-a-pomodoro-break/).

## How do you remember to take screen breaks?

You remember by letting something else keep time, because deep focus makes you forget. The best break reminder is one that goes off whether or not you feel like stopping.

1. **Tie breaks to your work blocks.** Work for 25 to 50 minutes, then break. A timer does the remembering.
2. **Set the next break before you start.** Decide now that the break comes at, say, 10:30.
3. **Make the break easy to start.** Keep a water bottle across the room, not on your desk, so getting a drink means standing up.
4. **Link breaks to things you already do.** Stand when you finish a call, or after you send a long message.
5. **Start small.** Two reliable short breaks beat a perfect plan you drop by Wednesday.

## What if you are in the middle of something?

If the timer rings mid-thought, write down where you are in one line, then take the break. Next time, you can pick up from that line instead of rebuilding the whole thought. This also works against the pull to "just finish this first", which is how a 25-minute block becomes a three-hour one.

If you often lose a great stretch of focus to breaks, try a slightly longer block rather than skipping the break. [Pomodoro vs 52/17 vs 90-minute blocks](/guides/pomodoro-vs-52-17-vs-90-minute-blocks/) compares rhythms with longer work and rest periods.

## What if your job is screens all day?

Some jobs mean eight hours at a screen, with meetings on screens too. A few changes help:

- **Make some meetings screen-free.** Take a call on your feet, with headphones, while walking.
- **Print or use paper for long reading** when you can, and make notes by hand.
- **Take lunch away from the desk.** [Lunch break when working from home](/guides/lunch-break-when-working-from-home/) has a simple routine, and the idea works in an office too.
- **Change your set-up.** Place the screen at a comfortable distance, keep the room lit, and avoid glare.

If your eyes are often sore, dry or blurry, or you get headaches after screen work, see an eye care professional. Breaks help, but they do not replace an eye test or the right glasses.

## Common questions

### How often should I take a break from my computer?

Look away from the screen every 20 to 30 minutes for at least 20 seconds, and take a longer 5 to 10 minute break every hour. Adjust the timing to how your eyes and body feel.

### Is the 20-20-20 rule enough?

It is a good habit for your eyes, but it does not cover your body or your mind. Pair it with standing up, moving and a proper longer break each hour.

### Is scrolling my phone a screen break?

No. Your eyes are still focused close up, and you take in new things to read. Put the phone away and look out of a window or walk around instead.

### Can I take a break from screens in a short work day?

Yes. A break of two minutes still helps, because the point is to change what your eyes, body and attention are doing, not to be away for long.

## In short

- A proper screen break is away from every screen, including your phone.
- Look away every 20 to 30 minutes and take a longer break each hour.
- Rest your eyes, move, drink water and keep the break finite.
- Let a timer keep time, and write a one-line note if you are mid-thought.
- See an eye care professional if your eyes are often sore or your vision blurs.
